# Updating Riser Chart Manager

*A one-pager to send along with a new build. Nothing here touches your charts — your singers and arrangements live in your `.riserchart` file, not in the app.*

---

## The short version

1. Save the new `Riser_Chart_Manager.html` **into the same folder as your old one**, replacing it.
2. Open it the way you always do.
3. Check the version badge at the top right — it should show the new number.

That's it. Your desktop shortcut, your charts, and your file all keep working.

---

## Step by step

**1. Save the new file over the old one.**

The new file has the same name as the one you already have: `Riser_Chart_Manager.html`. Put it in the same folder and let it replace the old copy. Windows will ask "do you want to replace this file?" — yes.

If your browser saves it as `Riser_Chart_Manager (1).html` instead, that's fine too, but rename it to `Riser_Chart_Manager.html` and replace the old one, so your shortcut keeps pointing at the current version.

**2. Open it.**

Double-click it, or use your usual desktop shortcut / pinned tab.

**3. Confirm the update landed.**

Look at the far right of the top bar (the small badge next to the ♥). There's a small rounded badge with the version number in it, next to the ♥. If it shows the new number, you're on the new build.

The first time you open a new version, a small **What's new** window appears listing what changed. Read it and click **Got it**. You can bring it back any time by clicking that version badge.

**4. Open your chart file.**

A blue bar across the top says *"You were last working in ..."* with your file name. Click **Open it** and you're back where you were. (Your browser has to ask permission to touch the file again after an update — that's the one click.)

---

## What if something looks wrong?

**"My charts are gone!"** They aren't. They're in your `.riserchart` file. Use **File ▾ → Open…** and pick it, or use the blue bar at the top. The app itself never stores your singers — that's on purpose, so updating it can't lose anything.

**"It still shows the old version number."** The new file didn't replace the old one. Check your Downloads folder for a copy with `(1)` in the name.

**"My browser says the file might be dangerous."** It's a plain web page, not a program — browsers say that about any downloaded `.html` file. Choose Keep.

---

## Before you update (optional, but nice)

Press **Ctrl+S** in the old version first, so your file is current. In the new version this happens by itself, but the old one needed asking.
