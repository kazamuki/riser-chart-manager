How it works

Quick start

Add singers with + Add singer (or bring a whole roster in via File → Import roster…), then drag them from the panel onto the riser spots. Drop a singer on an occupied spot and the two swap. Nothing needs saving as you work — every change goes into the chart you're editing, and once you've saved a .riserchart file, the file keeps itself up to date too. Watch the filename in the top bar: it tells you exactly where things stand.

The board

Drag chips between spots, back to the panel, or into the dashed Staging strip below the front row — a parking spot for singers you're actively re-arranging (its order is yours; it never re-sorts). The + / − buttons at the ends of every row add or remove a spot right on the board, and the curve reflows around the singers already placed; if a removed spot was occupied, its singer moves to Staging, never lost. Each chart has its own riser shape — spots you add or remove belong to the chart you’re editing, and your other charts are left alone (Setup → Riser shape can copy one shape to all of them). The Director slot at the front centre holds one singer: drag anyone in to show who’s directing this chart, and drag them out (or press its ×) to empty it. ⇋ Flip (top right of the board) mirrors the stage; the zoom buttons (bottom right) fit big choruses on small screens. A dashed center line marks the middle of the risers on the board and on printouts — toggle it under Chart ▾.

Reference images: paste a picture straight onto the board with Ctrl+V (⌘V on Mac) — or right-click the board and choose Paste image or Add image from file…. Handy for parking a screenshot of your attendance list beside the chart while you sort out who's coming. Drag it to move, drag the corner to resize, and use the × (or the Delete key) to remove it. The image belongs to the chart it's on and doesn't appear on printouts.

Charts

You are always editing a real chart, named in the selector at the top. Everything you change goes straight into that chart — there's nothing to remember to save, and switching charts never loses work. Use Chart ▾ → New chart… to start another one with empty risers, or Duplicate chart… to copy the one you're looking at (handy for building next month's show from this month's). Rename… and Delete… do what they say; you'll always keep at least one chart. Each chart stores who stands where, the shape of the risers, who’s directing, who’s coming, and any reference images — singer details live on the roster, so editing a name or photo updates every chart at once.

Attendance: press ✓ Attendance at the top right of the board to track who's coming to this performance. Click a singer to gray them out (not coming); click again to bring them back. The banner shows how many are coming, and grayed singers stay grayed on the printout too. Attendance is saved with each chart, so every performance keeps its own list. To compare against a roster you already have, paste a screenshot right onto the board (see below).

Roster, parts & groups

Double-click any chip to edit that singer — part, status, height (for the sightline helper), photo, notes (a ⚑ shows on their chip). The roster groups singers by part, one column per part as you scroll sideways; + Add singer, the All / On risers / On bench filters and the search box stay put in the bar above, however far across you scroll. Quartets and Balance live in the small switcher on the right of that bar. Setup (⚙) manages your parts (2–8, any names and colors), the riser shape, branding, and the optional quartets/small-groups view. File → Export roster writes a spreadsheet of everyone plus their spot in every chart — and it re-imports cleanly.

Printing

Print ▾ offers three layouts: the chart with a numbered name list, the chart alone (biggest on the page — great for taping to the riser rail), or a roster-only list grouped by part. Charts scale to fill a landscape page.

Your data

Everything lives in this one file and in .riserchart files on your computer — there's no account, no server, and nothing leaves your machine. Your work saves automatically in this browser between sessions, so it's here when you come back.

In Chrome and Edge, the first time you use File → Save the app remembers your file and keeps writing to it as you work. The filename in the top bar shows the state: Saved 3:42 PM means it's on disk, saving… means it's a second behind, and a ⚠ means a save failed and you should press Ctrl+S.

In Firefox and Safari, a web page isn't allowed to write to a file on your disk, so File → Download a copy… is exactly that — a snapshot in your Downloads folder, named with the date so copies stay in order. It does not update itself afterwards, and the top bar says so: copy saved 3:42 PM, or changes since that copy once you've done more work. Download a fresh copy whenever you want a file to keep, print from elsewhere, or send to someone.

Either way, the copy in this browser is only as safe as the browser. If yours is set to clear site data when it closes, download a copy before you finish for the day — the top bar will remind you once your newest copy is more than a week old.

When you open the app fresh, a bar at the top offers the file you last worked in — one click and you're back where you were.

Tip: this app is just a file — drag Riser Chart Manager.html onto your desktop (or pin its browser tab) and it opens like any other app. No install, ever.

Keyboard shortcuts

Ctrl+Z undo (up to 30 steps — placements, staging, spots, roster edits, attendance, images, chart switches and chart operations) · Ctrl+V paste an image onto the board · Delete removes the selected image · Ctrl+S save · Ctrl+Shift+S save as · Ctrl+O open · Esc closes any menu or window (and leaves attendance mode). On a Mac, use ⌘ instead of Ctrl.
